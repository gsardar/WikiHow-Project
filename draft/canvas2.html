Introduction

Defining the Scope

  • Diachronic Analysis: The study of a phenomenon as it evolves over time. In this study, I am ingtracking the evolution of wikiHow articles domains edited by the contributions of people al over the web across a 21-year timespan (2005–2026).
  • The Platform: wikiHow, a vast repository of collaborative human instruction, currently hosting over 258,000+ articles. Whereas I have been able to scrape the contributions of 4004 articles which is just 1.5% of the total articles.
  • The Focus: Examining the contributions of Admins, Boosters, Verified Authors, and Unregistered Contributors to uncover latent gender stereotypes and the evolution of gender-neutral language.

Motivation

The Systemic Disparity (Hill & Shaw)

  • Studies on Wikipedia demonstrate a stark demographic imbalance in collaborative knowledge production. Adjusted estimates revealed that the actual proportion of female US adult editors was only 22.7%, and the global proportion was 16.1%.
  • Researchers demonstrated that the decline in active users was not because the encyclopedia ran out of new topics to cover. Instead, the drop was driven by a sharp increase in newcomer rejection, which plummeted retention rates as the platform faced surges of low-quality edits.

The Core Questions

  • Is offline gender disparity carried over into online instructional spaces?
  • Do we see contributors engaging primarily in their respective gender-coded articles?

Theoretical Framework: The Task Continuums

Our methodology categorizes articles into macro and micro "Continuums." This framework maps tasks based on sociological gender-coding (from Female to Male).

Origin of the Framework

  • Computational Semantics: It has been seen in NLP debiasing studies that occupations are mapped in terms of male to female vectors (Bolukbasi et al., 2016). AI places words like "plumbing" or "boss" physically closer to the word "man," and words like "baking" or "receptionist" closer to the word "woman."
  • Human Baselines: Datasets like WinoBias quantify assumptions linking specific labor tasks to specific gender pronouns.Sentences containing pro-stereotypical links (e.g., "The doctor yelled at the nurse because he was stressed") and anti-stereotypical links ("The doctor yelled at the nurse because she was stressed").
  • Sociological Foundations: Grounded in the societal expectations of domestic versus occupational labor.

Objective & Hypothesis

Research Objective

To identify and quantify any form of gender disparity, stereotype enforcement, or ideological gatekeeping present within wikiHow's instructional articles.

Core Hypothesis

Stereotypes in the digital space of wikiHow are derived from the gender expectations in offline social reality.

  • Contributors will primarily engage in domains matching their own offline societal gender coding.
  • Cross-domain contributions (e.g., a female contributor writing in a highly technical male-coded domain) may face ideological obstruction and get rejected.

Classification of Contribution Types

To analyze gatekeeping objectively, we extract the exact textual "diff" between the accepted and reverted articles to bypass potentially biased human moderator tags.

Preliminary Tag Rules:

  • RCP: Recent Changes Patrol. A fast-paced tool used by Boosters/Admins to instantly roll back edits.
  • NAB: New Article Booster. Admins who gatekeep newly created pages.
  • TOC / HTOC: Table of Contents / Horizontal Table of Contents. (Stylistic maintenance flags).
  • ce / copyedit: Fixing grammar or spelling.
  • vand: Vandalism. Purely destructive editing.
  • NPOV: Neutral Point of View. Often used when enforcing policy, sometimes used to gatekeep highly opinionated or gender-specific advice.
  • NCA: New Contributor Alert.
  • TYSK: Things You Should Know (a specific WikiHow content block).

Proving the Hypothesis

1. Digital Spaces Mirror Offline Expectations

  • The Macro Distribution (Figure 4) and Demographic Gradients (Figure 5) conclusively prove that contributors self-segregate.
  • Women hold heavy majorities in the Domestic continuum (e.g., Baby Care, Baking), while Men hold heavy majorities in Technical/Occupational continuums.

2. Cross-Domain Obstruction Exists

  • The Perpetrator-Target Matrix (Figure 6) reveals high friction when boundaries are crossed. Male gatekeepers revert female-coded articles 3.8x more often than male-coded articles.
  • This friction results in the Chilling Effect (Figure 7), leading to faster account decay for women and non-binary users facing ideological gatekeeping.

Conclusion

  • Systemic Inheritance: wikiHow, despite its collaborative open-source design, inherits and amplifies offline systemic gender biases.
  • The Gatekeeper Problem: The moderation structure (Patrol/Gatekeepers) effectively enforces ideological boundaries, creating a hostile environment for cross-domain contribution.
  • Future Implications: Open-knowledge platforms must rethink their hierarchical permission structures. Without addressing algorithmic and human moderation bias, "neutral" knowledge repositories will remain demographically skewed.

Limitations & Future Implications

1. Limitations: Sample & Scale

The 3,958 articles analyzed were selected for high relevance to our continuums. While theoretically robust, this represents ~1.5% of wikiHow. Future focus will scale this to the remaining 98.5%.

2. Limitations: Classification Nuance

Deterministic (Regex) offers high surety but low reach. Probabilistic (GenAI/Genderize.io) relies on profile cues (names/bio) which may not reflect true self-identified gender. The goal is to shift toward behavioral context understanding.

3. Future Horizons

Expanding the project to include automated "Shadow Healer" workflows and improved domain-shifting tracking across the entire platform ecosystem.
