Abstract
The following research investigates the hidden layers of authority and gender stratification on wikiHow. For a long time, the site has been seen as a community-driven project where anyone can share knowledge. But our data reveals a very different story under the surface. We used a new algorithm to profile thousands of contributors and analyzed how their edits are treated by the platform admins. What we found is a sophisticated system of gatekeeping that often targets domestic and gendered knowledge. This paper shows how technical rules and automated bots are used to enforce a specific kind of professional tone that might be pushing out authentic voices, especially in female-coded categories.

Introduction
WikiHow is one of the most visited websites for practical advice in the world. It covers everything from how to fix a leaky pipe to how to handle a difficult relationship. On the surface, it looks like a standard wiki where anyone can edit. However, the platform has evolved a complex hierarchy of users including Boosters and Administrators who have enormous power. Our research goal was to understand if this power is used fairly or if it creates a glass ceiling for certain types of contributors. We decided to focus on four "continuums" of knowledge—domestic, occupational, entertainment, and policy—to see if the gatekeeping patterns changed based on the topic.

Methodology and Technical Approach
To carry out this analysis we built a custom pipeline that uses large language models as a demographic attribution algorithm. This allowed us to infer the gender and social standing of contributors with high accuracy without needing private data. We also developed a "Gatekeeping Matrix" to track moments of conflict in the article histories. Instead of just looking at who edited what, we looked at why edits were rejected. We focused on unified diffs between user contributions and administrator reverts. This technical setup allowed us to see exactly what content was being "policed" and the speed at which it happened.

Observations on Administrative Gatekeeping
During our crawl of over 11,000 edit summaries, we noticed several strong patterns that define the wikiHow authority structure. One of the most common is the use of the Recent Changes Patrol, or RCP. This is a tool that allows admins to revert edits almost instantly. We saw that many genuine contributions are rejected within seconds. The reason for this fast rejection is likely administrative efficiency. Admins are under pressure to keep the site clean, so they use quick pattern matching instead of deep reading. 

Another observation is the use of automated bots like MiscBot to carry out reverts. This creates a kind of administrative distance where the rejection feels like a neutral technical error rather than a human judgment call. We also found that admins are obsessed with "professional formatting" like green boxes and table of contents. Even when a user provides excellent expert advice, it is often reverted if it does not fit the corporate style of the site. This suggests that wikiHow is prioritizing visual consistency over the actual quality of advice. This "Professionalization of Domestic Knowledge" is a key part of how gatekeeping works on the platform.

Hypothesis on Gender Stratification
Our initial data suggests that the "Neutrality Policy" or NPOV is being used as a tool for gender policing. When contributors add nuanced gendered advice—such as specific tips for mothers or women—it is often flagged as biased and removed. This effectively treats traditional feminine knowledge as a form of "opinion" while treating professional or male-coded advice as "objective." We plan to prove this in the next stage of our project by generating time-series graphs that show the promotion velocity of men compared to women and the rejection rates of gendered content across the different continuums.
